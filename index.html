READ THIS FIRST

We make content that gets watched or we make nothing.

This is the whole job in one page. Everything after it is detail.

$1M / month
REVENUE · THE TARGET

That is the number this whole operation is built to hit. Every rule, every quota and every workflow in this document is reverse-engineered from it. Views are the mechanism; this is the point.

THE JOB

You are the backbone of the whole thing. Every account, every follower, every dollar of inbound revenue sits on top of what comes out of your hands. There is nothing underneath you holding it up.

The target is millions of views, not thousands. Every niche we are in has someone else in it doing this right now, and the only thing between us and them is what we put out this week.

It is not only production. You are expected to think — new concepts, new directions, new moves, and how the dashboard itself should work. If something is slow or stupid, say so. Everyone's mind and everyone's words matter here, and the best calls we have made came from every seat in the room.

The five rules

1 — Always ask questions

Why do we do it like this? Can we not change it? Let's try this. I don't know how to do this, help. How do we do this?

All of it matters and it matters a lot. Nobody loses anything by asking. The only expensive thing is a week of work built on a guess nobody checked.

2 — She must feel real

Every single piece of content must feel real. Any signal of AI is terrible. Get used to creating AI content that feels real — the speech, the small things in the background, fake logos, anything.

People see this, it ruins credibility, and it can kill a model quick.

3 — Every second of a video matters

Hook, lead, CTA. If a second does none of those three jobs, cut it.

4 — The niche decides everything

She is not a girl. She is an F1 girl, a flight attendant, a cosplayer, a construction girl. Content that could belong to any account belongs to no account.

And the niche only holds if she feels real inside it. On-niche content that reads as generated is worth nothing — see rule 2.

5 — Reference images are the foundation

Every picture and every video is built off them. A weak reference set poisons months of output and nobody notices until the account is dead. Get these right before you make anything else.

How your day is built

Tasks land in The Line. Each one has a weight. A full day is a selection of them, split by importance.

A task might be “make 40 videos for Kira today” — how you get there is yours. It might be “recreate these exact five, one to one” — then it is exactly those five. Quality is on you.

Standing volume rule

  • Make ~2 variations of every video type you produce. One version is a guess. Two is a test.
  • Always generate more than you need. You have two eyes and one opinion — you will pick wrong sometimes.
  • After week one the standing quota is 40 successful videos and 20 carousel images a day. See the bar →

FOUNDATIONS

Anatomy of a video

Three jobs. Every second belongs to one of them or it gets cut.

Hook

Buys attention. Text on screen, a face mid-motion, a line you have to finish hearing. It happens inside the first second — not the first three.

Lead

Keeps attention. Delivers whatever the hook promised: the joke, the reveal, the information, the look. Dead air here kills the video.

CTA

Cashes attention in. Two forms:

  • Explicit — comment this, follow for part two, tap the profile. Works after you have given real value: a laugh, information, dopamine.
  • Implicit — the whole video is the CTA. It retains or polarises hard enough that people comment and check the account without being asked. This is the higher ceiling.
FAILURE MODE

A video with a hook and a lead and no CTA gets views and no followers. A video with a CTA and a weak hook gets nothing at all. In that order.

The feeling

Every video that goes viral has a methodology behind it. You will not get it from a list. You get it by making enough of them that you develop the feel, and then you have it for good.

If you have ever been scrolling and thought why am I still watching this — that is a good fucking video. Work out what it did to you. Learn to build hooks that do the same thing to someone else, and you stop being the person scrolling and become the person behind the viral account.

CONTENT TYPE 01

Reference images

The images used to generate every picture and every video of a model. The most important asset in onboarding, and the one people rush.

What a reference set must be

Face

  • Clear, unobstructed, well lit. No crop, no hair across it, no extreme angle.
  • Either her real face, or a consistent altered face she has approved — pick one and lock it forever.
  • One image is the hero: highest quality, most neutral, used as the identity anchor on nearly every generation. The rest are add-ons.

Body

  • Full body in the frame. Half-body references produce broken generations.
  • Consistent proportions across the whole set. If she changes shape between images, the AI will change her shape in the output.

Niche coverage

  • One reference for every setting or experience the niche can produce.
  • Cosplayer → several costumes. Flight attendant → uniform, layover, off-duty. Construction girl → site, van, home. F1 girl → paddock, garage, grid.
  • If you cannot name what she is doing in the image, the image is not covering anything.

Feel

  • Candid and human. She is a person, not a product render. Slight imperfection reads as real.
  • Sexy is not optional. It is the reason the account exists.
  • Variation in outfit, setting and expression across the set — same person, different moments.

Real face vs altered face

REAL FACEALTERED FACE
WhenShe is fine appearing as herself.She is not. You build a face close to hers that she approves.
DifficultyLow. The face is already consistent.High. Every generation drifts a little; the drift compounds.
RuleUse her real material as widely as possible.Approve the face once, then never regenerate it from scratch. Always build from the locked hero image.
THE CONSTRAINT NOBODY GETS AROUND

Every model sends different source photos, in different outfits, in different settings, at different quality. You work with what she gives you. There is no standard input, so build the set around her material instead of forcing a template onto it.

CONTENT TYPE 02

Posts & carousels

Her day to day. An inside look at her life, and a clear look at her. This is what a stranger judges the whole account on.

The three rules

1 — She is doing something

A niche activity, with a light showing of a hobby. Not posing. Doing.

2 — It tells a story

You can say what is happening the instant you see it. If you have to think, the viewer has already scrolled.

3 — It leads on to something observable

There is a next thing. A place she is going, a thing she is about to do, a person off-frame. It opens rather than closes.

They should be interesting and attractive at the same time. Never one without the other.

THE MISTAKE

Normal photos of her doing nothing in particular. She is not anyone — she is a specific person with a specific life. Generic stills make a generic account, and a generic account earns nothing.

Sourcing — where the friction lives

  • Source images come from Pinterest. We do not use the models' real photos for posts.
  • Pick source images where the subject already looks like the model. Less to fix, less drift.
  • Pick source images where the niche is already present. Building an F1 lifestyle carousel out of a League of Legends cosplay still is a fight you chose. A girl who rides motorbikes, in a room full of helmets, is free.
  • Reference images and reference videos are everything. A bad reference costs an hour and still comes out wrong.

The two prompts

LIFESTYLE POST · HER FACE ON ANOTHER PHOTO
put face of girl one onto face of girl 2

maintain photo 2 photo quality and outfit or subject, keep it natural
NICHE LIFESTYLE POST · FACE + OUTFIT ONTO ANOTHER PHOTO
put subject identity of girl one onto subject identity of person 2

maintain girl 1 outfit

maintain photo 2 photo quality and setting

*keep it natural*

Write the prompt to fit the output you already decided on. Do not generate first and decide after.

WARDROBE

Mix her niche outfit with ordinary day-to-day clothes. Nobody wears the same thing or posts the same kind of picture every single day, and an account that does looks like a bot.

Settings that work

  • Batch at 3 / 16 — the highest hit rate we have found.
  • Friend images perform. They express a life outside the camera and read as wholesome, which buys you room to be sexy elsewhere.
  • Fresh and specifically different scores extra. Common girl-posts are the floor, not the target.

CONTENT TYPE 03

Recreated videos

A viral video, rebuilt with our model as the subject. The idea is proven before you start. This is the core skill and half of everything you ship.

What it is

  • A reference video goes into Seedance or Wan with the model's reference image. Only the character changes.
  • Spot a viral that relates to the niche → you can recreate it around 80% of the time. The failure cases are format, not idea: heavy cuts, many subjects, extreme camera work.
  • The original already earned its views. You are borrowing proof, not guessing.
  • It does not have to be someone else's video. One of our own that performed is a reference video like any other — recreate our winners across models and niches.
PER MODEL, NOT PER LIBRARY

Every model is a different person, in a different niche, with a different ICP. One model's reference video is not another's. Some are general or funny enough to overlap — most are not. Never batch the same reference across the roster without checking it.

Audio

  • The recreated render usually has the wrong sound, or none.
  • Standard fix: take it into CapCut and stitch the new render over the original video so the original audio survives.
  • You can also build audio from scratch. That is a choice, not a requirement.

The split

STANDING RULE

50% recreated / 50% fresh. Proven is proven — it performed once and will most likely perform again. The other half has to be fresh because fresh is how new formats get tested, and a tested fresh video becomes next month's proven one.

CONTENT TYPE 04

Fresh videos / capcut videos

New videos, assembled from clips you generate, cut in CapCut. Factory makes the pieces. CapCut makes the video.

Where fresh videos come from

  • A proven concept you want to remake rather than copy — same idea, our angle.
  • An old video that needed rework — right idea, wrong execution.
  • A completely fresh concept nobody has made for the niche.

One clip or many. The number of clips is a consequence of the idea, never the starting point.

The pieces

EDITOR VS CAPCUT

The Editor is a hammer. CapCut is a multi-tool. The hammer is fast and it does its one job well — a simple talking video showing her personality or interests, captioned, music on, sent. It cannot create anything that isn't already in the clip. When the video has to be built, you are in CapCut.

Why this is the frontier

  • Recreations prove what works. Fresh videos are how you make something that has never been done for the niche.
  • Text, sound and filters used differently from the standard talking-head is where the reach is.
  • Together, recreations and fresh videos are what currently drives engagement and inbound revenue. Everything else supports them.

WALKTHROUGH · WEIGHT 1

Make reference images

Slow work with permanent consequences. Do it once, properly.

  1. Decide the faceReal face, or an altered face she is comfortable with. If altered: build it, get it approved, then treat it as fixed. Never regenerate the face from scratch again.
  2. Read the niche before you open anythingList every setting and experience the niche produces. That list is your shot list. A flight attendant has a uniform, a layover, an off-duty day. Cover all of it.
  3. Take her real images into Nano BananaUse what she actually sent. Put the right outfit for the niche and her personality onto her.
  4. Edit for full face and full outfitFace clear and unobstructed. Whole outfit visible. Full body in frame. Anything less is not a reference image.
  5. Generate 5–6 minimum — then moreAlways overproduce. You have two eyes and one opinion. The set you keep should be chosen from a set you rejected.
  6. Get a second pair of eyesConsistency errors are invisible to the person who made them. One or more people verify identity across the set before it goes in. This step is not optional on an altered face.
  7. Pick the heroOne high-quality, consistent image becomes the face anchor used on nearly every generation, especially images. Everything else is variation: outfit, setting, expression.
  8. Load them into FactoryReference section, under the model name and the correct niche. They appear and are usable immediately.
ALTERED FACES

Inconsistency compounds fast. Every generation drifts a little from the last, and drifts stack. Always build from the locked hero, never from the last output.

WALKTHROUGH · WEIGHT 1

Make a carousel

Batch work. Speed comes fast, but only after the source selection is right.

  1. Decide the output firstWhat day is she having? What is she doing? Write the prompt to fit that, not the other way round.
  2. Find source images on PinterestThings she would plausibly post. Prioritise sources where the subject already resembles her and the setting already fits the niche. This one choice decides how hard the rest is.
  3. Load the reference image, then the Pinterest imageReference first, source second. Use the right prompt for the job:
HER FACE ONLY
put face of girl one onto face of girl 2

maintain photo 2 photo quality and outfit or subject, keep it natural
HER FACE + HER OUTFIT
put subject identity of girl one onto subject identity of person 2

maintain girl 1 outfit

maintain photo 2 photo quality and setting

*keep it natural*
  1. Run the batch at 3 / 16Best hit rate. Generate the batch, then pick.
  2. Vary the wardrobe across the setMix the niche outfit with normal day-to-day clothes. Same outfit in every image reads as fake.
  3. Select and groupClick Select, choose the images that belong to one post, group them.
  4. Fill it with lifeUse the Lifestyle tab to add her daily activity to the carousel — her room, her collectibles, her city, her friends. This is what makes it a life instead of a photoshoot.
  5. Ready to postSelect the correct model and the correct niche. Check both. It goes to the Library where the scheduler picks it up.

WALKTHROUGH · WEIGHT 2

Factory videos from the prompt bank

Two entry points. Pick based on whether the idea already exists.

Route A — fresh idea, start at Scriptwriter

  1. Open ScriptwriterTag the model with @ — name and niche. It cannot write for a persona it does not know.
  2. Talk it through in detailBe specific about what you want. Vague in, vague out. Describe the shot, the action, the tone, the length.
  3. Send to FactorySelect the correct reference images for the video, then generate.
  4. Judge the renderIt may be right first time. Often it is not.
  5. Iterate properlyCopy the prompt, go back to the conversation where it was written, and explain the pros, the cons, and the specific change you want. Regenerate. Repeat until it is what you asked for — not until you are tired.

Route B — known format, start at the prompt bank

  1. Pick the promptPrompt bank holds prompts that already produced results.
  2. Add reference imagesCorrect model, correct niche.
  3. GenerateThe prompt comes attached — nothing else to write.

Route C — adapt a banked prompt to another niche

  1. Copy the prompt from the bank
  2. Go to Scriptwriter and tag the model it is forPaste it in.
  3. Describe the changesDifferent niche, different lines, different setting. Scriptwriter rewrites it.
  4. Send to Factory, pick references, generateSame finish as Route A.

WALKTHROUGH · WEIGHT 2 · MOST IMPORTANT

Recreate a video from the Ideas tab

Learn this one until it is automatic. It is half of everything you ship.

  1. Open Ideas and pick the modelIdeas are per model because niches and ICPs are per model. What works for one may be worthless for another.
  2. Pick an idea and copy the link
  3. Download the reference videoUse a safe downloader.
  4. Load the reference video + the model's reference imageThe image decides who she is. The video decides everything else.
  5. Write the swap promptStart from the base below and change it to fit the video in front of you. Every video is different.
BASE SWAP PROMPT · EDIT PER VIDEO
Replace the woman in the video with the reference character.
Preserve the exact body movement, facial performance, camera movement,
framing, timing, environment and lighting.
Change only the character.
  1. Generate and check the swapMovement, timing and identity must all survive. If any one of them breaks, the video is not usable — fix the prompt, not the edit.
  2. Fix the audio in CapCutRecreations usually come back with different sound. Stitch the new render over the original video so the original audio is kept. Or build new audio if the idea calls for it — your call.
  3. Upload to the LibraryCorrect model, correct niche, Facebook and Instagram. It was made outside the Factory, so it will not appear on its own.
WHAT RECREATES AND WHAT DOESN'T
  • Recreates well: single subject, continuous shot, readable movement, clear framing.
  • Fights you: many hard cuts, multiple people, extreme camera movement, heavy effects.
  • Around 8 in 10 niche-relevant virals are recreatable. Do not force the other 2 — rebuild them as a fresh video instead.

WALKTHROUGH · WEIGHT 3

Fresh video in CapCut

The broadest task here. The process depends entirely on the video you decided to make — so decide first.

  1. Define the finished video before you generate anythingWhat happens, in what order, for how long. Clip count falls out of this. Skipping this step is why a fresh video takes all day.
  2. Generate every clip in the FactorySame process as any Factory video — Scriptwriter or prompt bank, correct references, iterate until each clip is right.
  3. Get every clip right before you editEditing does not rescue a bad clip. Regenerate it.
  4. Build it in CapCutText, sound, filters, stacking, timing. This is where a video becomes a video.
  5. Cut against hook / lead / CTAEvery second serves one of the three. If it serves none, delete it.
  6. Upload to the LibraryModel, niche, platforms. Built outside the Factory, so it will not appear on its own.
HOW YOU GET GOOD AT THIS

CapCut is a learn-by-doing skill. Nobody has ever read their way to it. Get your hands dirty, build badly, build again.

Week one & the bar

Week one is paid learning time. After that you are measured on output, and the numbers are not soft.

The daily bar

40
SUCCESSFUL VIDEOS / DAY

Successful is the operative word. A render that gets rejected in review did not happen. 40 shipped, not 40 attempted.

20
CAROUSEL IMAGES / DAY

Grouped, filled with Lifestyle, filed under the correct model and niche. Loose images sitting in a batch are not output.

What the numbers actually require

  • 40 videos = 20 recreated + 20 fresh. That is the 50/50 split at volume. You do not hit 40 by building 40 from scratch — you hit it by being fast at recreation, which pays for the time the fresh half costs you.
  • Batch, don't drip. Queue generations, work on edits while they run. Waiting on a render with nothing else open is where the day is lost.
  • Reject faster. A bad render is a sunk cost after ten seconds of looking at it. Regenerate and move.
  • Pull targets in bulk. One Spy and Ideas session at the start of the day beats hunting for a reference video 28 separate times.
  • File as you go. Uploading 40 videos to the Library at 11pm is how model and niche get set wrong.

What you can do by the end of week one

Make high quality videos

They represent the niche and read as a real life the model is actually living — not clips of a character.

Make high quality pictures

They reflect the character and the avatar. Someone who has never seen the account can describe her from the grid.

Ideate

You come up with new concepts and directions without being handed them. You add to the Ideas tab instead of only taking from it.

Spot value

You identify high value concepts and production opportunities on your own — you can look at a viral and immediately say whether it is recreatable, for which model, and why.
